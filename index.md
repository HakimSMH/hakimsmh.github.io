<section class="intro" markdown="1">

I design brain-computer interfaces and immersive systems that sense how their users perceive and feel. My work uses EEG to detect, in real time, states such as cybersickness, vection or perceived errors, and to adapt the interaction accordingly.
{: .lead }

I am an Associate Professor of Computer Science at the **University of Lille**. I conduct my research in the [BCI team](https://bci.univ-lille.fr/en/) of the [CRIStAL laboratory](https://www.cristal.univ-lille.fr) (UMR CNRS 9189) and teach in the EEA department (Electronics, Electrical Engineering and Automatic Control) of the Faculty of Science and Technology (FST). My research lies at the crossroads of brain-computer interfaces, immersive technologies and human-computer interaction, combining interaction design with physiological signal processing and machine learning. Applications range from virtual and augmented reality to assistive communication for people with severe motor impairments. I am also interested in the ethical and legal questions raised by neurotechnologies.

Since 2026, I have been **Deputy Director** of the CNRS Research Federation [Visual Sciences and Digital Cultures](https://fr-scv.univ-lille.fr) (SCV) and **Head of the BCI & Neuroprosthetics Axis** of the Lille NeurotechS Institute. I coordinated the European CHIST-ERA project [GENESIS](https://www.chistera.eu/projects/genesis) (2022–2025) and lead a work package of the LOOP project on closed-loop neurotechnologies. I serve as **Secretary General** of [CORTICO](https://www.cortico.fr), the French BCI research collective, and as a program committee member of IEEE VR and IEEE ISMAR. I am also domain leader for “Technology” in the European Master of Neurotechnology of [NeurotechEU](https://theneurotech.eu).

I received my PhD from Inria and INSA Rennes in 2019 (1st PhD Thesis Prize, Fondation Rennes 1), after research stays at TU Graz and Microsoft Research, followed by a postdoc at Inria.
